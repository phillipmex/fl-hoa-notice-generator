Florida HOA Fine Limits and HB 1203 (2024)

What the fine-limit statute says, what HB 1203 changed in 2024, and what a compliant violation notice needs to include.

The basic fine limit: s. 720.305(2), Florida Statutes

Under s. 720.305(2), Florida Statutes, a homeowners' association fine may not exceed $100 per violation, up to $1,000 in the aggregate for a continuing violation — unless the association's governing documents provide otherwise. That last clause matters: many declarations authorize higher fines, so the statutory cap is a floor-level default, not a universal ceiling. The first place to check is your own declaration and bylaws, not just the statute.

Verify before relying on this: fine figures and statutory cross-references change over time. Confirm the current text of s. 720.305, F.S., at the Florida Legislature's Online Sunshine site, and confirm what your own governing documents say about fine amounts, before a board acts on any number.

What HB 1203 (2024) added

HB 1203, passed in 2024, amended s. 720.305 to tighten what a fining or suspension notice must contain and how the hearing process must run. Based on the framework this tool's violation-notice generator is built around, the changes include:

  • Notice content: a fining notice must include a description of the violation, the specific action required to cure it (if the violation can be cured), and the hearing's date, location, and access details.
  • Hearing timing: at least 14 days' written notice of the hearing is required, and HB 1203 added a requirement that the hearing be held within 90 days of the notice.
  • Independent committee: the hearing must be before a committee of members who are not officers, directors, or employees of the association, or the spouse, parent, child, brother, or sister of an officer, director, or employee. If the committee does not approve the fine or suspension by majority vote, it may not be imposed.
  • Payment deadline after approval: HB 1203 added a deadline for paying an approved fine — understood as 30 days after the notice of the committee's approval.
Verify before relying on this: the items above reflect this tool's understanding of s. 720.305, F.S., as amended by HB 1203 (2024) at drafting time. Confirm the current statutory text — particularly the 90-day hearing window and the 30-day payment deadline — before a board relies on these figures.

Why the hearing step is not optional

A fine or suspension cannot be imposed on the strength of a violation notice alone. The owner is entitled to notice and an opportunity for a hearing before the independent committee described above. Skipping the hearing, using board members or employees on the committee, or failing to give the required notice period are the kinds of defects that put a fine at risk of being unenforceable — separately from whether the underlying violation was real.
