Florida HOA Board Meeting Notice Requirements

When 48 hours' posted notice is enough for a board meeting, and when it isn't, under s. 720.303(2), Florida Statutes.

Not legal advice
This page is general information about Chapter 720, Florida Statutes, as understood at the time of writing. It is not legal advice, and no attorney-client relationship is created. Verify current statute text before a board relies on any timing described below, and check your own governing documents, which may require more than the statutory minimum.

The default: 48 hours, posted conspicuously

Under s. 720.303(2), Florida Statutes, the ordinary rule for a board meeting is a notice posted in a conspicuous place within the community at least 48 hours in advance, with an exception for emergencies. That posting is generally enough on its own for a routine board meeting — no mailing to individual members is required for the ordinary case.

Verify before relying on this: confirm the current text of s. 720.303(2), F.S., and check whether your governing documents require additional notice methods (for example, posting to a community website or app) beyond the statutory conspicuous-place posting.

When 48 hours is not enough

Certain board meetings require more than the 48-hour posted notice. Meetings at which the board will consider special assessments or amendments to rules regarding parcel use require notice mailed or delivered to the members in advance — understood as 14 days — with additional content requirements beyond a simple posting. If either of those topics is on the agenda, a conspicuous-place posting alone is not a substitute for that mailed notice.

Assessment meetings need to say so

When a meeting will consider assessments, the notice of that meeting must state that assessments will be considered and describe the nature of the assessments — a generic agenda line is not enough. Members are entitled to know, from the notice itself, that money is on the table and roughly what for.

Verify before relying on this: the 14-day mailed-notice figure and the assessment-notice content requirement above reflect this tool's understanding of s. 720.303(2), F.S., at drafting time. Confirm current statute text before a board relies on either.

A practical checklist before posting

  • Is a special assessment or a parcel-use rule amendment on the agenda? If so, the 48-hour posting alone is not enough — plan for mailed/delivered notice well in advance.
  • Does the notice state the date, time, and location (plus phone/video access details, if offered)?
  • If assessments will be considered, does the notice say so and describe their nature?
  • Is the notice actually posted in a conspicuous place within the community — not just drafted?
  • Do your governing documents require anything beyond what the statute requires?
